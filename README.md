**<ins>Problem Statement</ins>**
The film industry is a high-risk business. Studios and investors commit large budgets to projects without knowing whether they will earn a return or be well received. For 2013 releases, it is unclear which factors (genre, budget, language, production company or country, runtime, release timing) are associated with commercial success (revenue, profit) and audience reception (ratings, popularity). Without this understanding, decisions on what to produce, how much to spend, and when to release are driven by intuition rather than evidence.

**<ins>Objective</ins>**
To analyze the 2013 movie metadata and identify the key drivers of box-office performance, profitability and audience rating, so stakeholders can make better production, budgeting and release decisions.

**<ins>Project Description</ins>**
This project analyzes 1,887 movies released in 2013 to understand what drives box-office success and audience ratings. The dataset covers each movie's budget, revenue, ratings, popularity, genre, language, production companies and countries, runtime and release date. After cleaning the data (including the many missing budget and revenue values), the analysis explores the 2013 market, measures profitability for movies with reliable financials, and examines how budget, genre, release timing and franchise status relate to revenue and ratings. The results are presented as visualizations and practical insights for production and release decisions.
